Part I · Foundations · Module 01

Why the names

By the end you can put Well, Spring, Stream, River and Sea in order, and take a point name like Shaoshang or Quchi apart without guessing.

Start with the image the classical texts use: a channel is a river. At the fingers and toes it is narrow, close to the surface, and easy to feel. As it travels toward the elbow or knee it gathers, widens, runs deeper, and finally sinks into the joint. The five Shu categories are five stages on that journey. Every channel has one of each, always in the same order from the extremity inward — a channel's five Shu points, sixty across the twelve channels.

Two words appear in the cards below. Qi is the flow a channel carries, and the five stations describe what that flow is doing. A cun is a proportional measure rather than a fixed length: one cun is about the width of your own thumb, so the same figure fits a child and an adult.

In one line

The station is fixed; the element is not. Every channel runs Well → Spring → Stream → River → Sea. Which element lands on which station follows a single rule, and module 2 gives it.

Two categories share the pinyin jing: the Well 井 and the River 經. The atlas keeps the English attached, Jing-Well and Jing-River, so a spoken instruction cannot send you to the wrong end of the channel.

Three names to read

LU11Shaoshang 少商

少 lesser + 商 the Metal note. A small Metal point at the thumb tip, on the Metal Lung channel. The name points at both size and element.

LI11Quchi 曲池

曲 bend + 池 pool. It sits at the lateral end of the elbow crease, where the channel bends around the joint. A pool in the bend.

TB10Tianjing 天井

天 heaven + 井 well. A well set high on the arm, in the hollow just above the olecranon — the point of the elbow. The name is a location joke: a well in the sky.

These readings are memory aids, not a dictionary. Names shifted across centuries and references gloss them differently. The textbook location is the authority: use the name to recall the picture, then confirm the landmark.

Part I · Foundations · Module 02

The element cycle

By the end you can walk both cycles in either direction, and rebuild every Shu point's element from the channel's polarity and element.

Two element facts sit side by side in this atlas. A channel belongs to one element: the Lung channel is Metal. A Shu point carries an element of its own: LU11 is Wood even though the Lung is Metal. Keep the two facts separate. They meet at one point per channel, the horary point, where the point's element matches the channel's element.

The five phases are processes, not five substances in jars. Each names a kind of movement:

  • Wood rises and expands: spring, germination, the push upward.
  • Fire flares and warms: summer, full expression.
  • Earth settles and transforms: late summer, the still centre between seasons.
  • Metal contracts and refines: autumn, the clean edge, taking in and letting go.
  • Water stores and descends: winter, depth, the reserve that feeds the next spring.

The generating cycle · 生

  • Wood feeds →
  • Fire makes →
  • Earth bears →
  • Metal carries →
  • Water nourishes →

In full: Wood feeds Fire, Fire makes Earth, Earth bears Metal, Metal carries Water, Water nourishes Wood. This is the cycle the Shu points follow across the limb. It is also the cycle a practitioner means by “generating” or sheng 生.

The controlling cycle · 克

  • Wood parts →
  • Earth dams →
  • Water quenches →
  • Fire melts →
  • Metal cuts →

In full: Wood parts Earth, Earth dams Water, Water quenches Fire, Fire melts Metal, Metal cuts Wood. Read it as a star across the generating ring: each phase controls the phase two steps ahead. Wood is two steps before Earth; Fire two before Metal; Earth two before Water; Metal two before Wood; Water two before Fire.

Keep 克 as the other half of the classical picture: it explains why a phase can fall out of balance, not where a point sits. The Shu arithmetic from here walks only the generating cycle.

One flip, two sequences

Every channel is one half of a pair. The yin channel runs the inner, shaded route of the limb and the yang channel the outer, exposed route: Lung with Large Intestine, Spleen with Stomach, Liver with Gallbladder. Which half a channel is — its polarity — decides the element on each station. Yin channels start at Wood on the Well. Yang channels start at Metal on the Well. Both then walk the generating cycle across the five stations.

Derive a whole channel

Take the Lung: yin, and its own element is Metal. Yin starts at Wood, so walk the generating cycle across the stations. The horary point is where the point element matches the channel element.

Lung · Yin · Metal
LU11 Well · Wood→LU10 Spring · Fire→LU9 Stream · Earth→LU8 River · Metal→LU5 Sea · Water

Metal lands on the River, so LU8 Jingqu is the horary point. The channel is Metal; the River station is Metal; they meet.

Now the Gallbladder: yang, and its own element is Wood. Yang starts at Metal at the Well.

Gallbladder · Yang · Wood
GB44 Well · Metal→GB43 Spring · Water→GB41 Stream · Wood→GB38 River · Fire→GB34 Sea · Earth

Wood lands on the Stream, so GB41 Zulinqi is the horary point. The Lung's target was Metal; the Gallbladder's is Wood. The method is identical — only the element you are looking for changes.

Why the flip

Because one sequence starts at Wood and the other at Metal, a yin channel and its paired yang channel never carry the same element at the same station. LU11 is Wood; LI1 is Metal. LU8 is Metal; LI5 is Fire. The pair is complementary, not duplicated.

Part II · The five elements · Module 03

木Wood

By the end you can say where Wood lands on the Liver and the Gallbladder, and why the two stations differ.

Wood is the rising phase: spring, germination, the push upward and outward. Its channels are the Liver (yin) and Gallbladder (yang). Both channels belong to Wood, but their points do not carry the same elements at the same stations.

On the yin Liver the sequence starts at Wood, so Wood is the Well: LR1 Dadun, at the big-toe nail. On the yang Gallbladder the sequence starts at Metal, so Wood arrives two stations later at the Stream: GB41 Zulinqi, on the foot. Those are the two Wood horary points.

Part II · The five elements · Module 04

火Fire

By the end you can name all four Fire channels and predict where Fire lands on each of them.

Fire is the flaring phase: summer, warmth, full expression. It is the only phase with four channels, two yin-yang pairs. The Heart and Small Intestine are the classical pair; the Pericardium and Triple Burner mirror them on the arm.

On both yin channels Fire lands at the Spring: HT8 Shaofu and PC8 Laogong, both on the palm. On both yang channels it lands at the River: SI5 Yanggu at the wrist and TB6 Zhigou on the forearm. That symmetry is a useful check. If you know one Fire channel, you can predict the station on the other three.

金Metal

By the end you can place Metal on the Lung and Large Intestine, and explain why this phase breaks the “own point sits farther in” pattern.

Metal is the contracting phase: autumn, the clean edge, taking in and refining. Its channels are the Lung (yin) and Large Intestine (yang). Part III · The body map · Module 08

Fingertip to elbow

By the end you can find any of the sixty points on your own body from its station alone, without reading a code.

The element chapters told you which point. This chapter tells you where. Because the five stations repeat on every channel, there are only five landmarks to learn, and each one has a hand version and a foot version. Learn the landmark and you can find a point you have never memorised.

Station 01

Well

A fingertip or toe tip, about 0.1 cun from the corner of the nail.

Station 02

Spring

Between two digits, or on the palmar side of the hand.

Station 03

Stream

The wrist or ankle joint line — and the knuckles, on three yang hand channels.

Station 04

River

The forearm or calf, 1 to 4 cun in from the crease.

Station 05

Sea

The elbow or knee: on the crease, or in the hollow just below it.

井Jing-Well · the extremity

At the tip of a finger or toe, about 0.1 cun from a nail corner. Two wells break that rule: PC9 sits at the centre of the middle fingertip, and KI1 sits on the sole of the foot, in the hollow that appears when the toes curl.

HandLU11LI1HT9SI1PC9TB1
FootSP1ST45KI1BL67LR1GB44

The colour on each code is the channel's element, not the point's. Every yin well is Wood and every yang well is Metal, whichever channel it sits on — so on the yin rows the colour and the point's own element deliberately disagree.

滎Ying-Spring · the web

Where two digits meet: between the fingers, or between the toes. On the hand the yin channels — LU10, HT8 and PC8 — sit on the palmar side, in the hollow a fingertip finds in a loose fist.

HandLU10LI2HT8SI2PC8TB2
FootSP2ST44KI2BL66LR2GB43

The spring is the first station in from the tip, and the easiest to miss: the web is a crease, and the point sits just proximal to it, at the border of red and white skin.

輸Shu-Stream · the joint line

The wrist or ankle. On the three yin hand channels the Stream sits on the wrist crease; on the three yang hand channels it sits one step earlier, at the knuckles — LI3, SI3 and TB3.

HandLU9LI3HT7SI3PC7TB3
FootSP3ST43KI3BL65LR3GB41

On a yin channel the Stream is also the Yuan-Source point, the one most references list first — LU9, HT7, PC7, SP3, KI3, LR3. That double duty is why these six appear on almost every formula sheet.

經Jing-River · the long bone

On the forearm or calf, one to four cun in from the crease, in the gap between bone and tendon. On the leg several cluster right at a malleolus, where the channel rounds the ankle: SP5, ST41, BL60 and LR4.

HandLU8LI5HT4SI5PC5TB6
FootSP5ST41KI7BL60LR4GB38

Rivers are where the channel runs over a long straight stretch, so the point is a dip between two structures rather than a crease. LI5 sits in the anatomical snuffbox; KI7 sits two cun above KI3, in front of the heel tendon.

合He-Sea · the joint

The elbow or knee, on the crease or in the hollow beside it. Two sit slightly lower: ST36 is 3 cun below the kneecap, and GB34 just below the head of the fibula.

HandLU5LI11HT3SI8PC3TB10
FootSP9ST36KI10BL40LR8GB34

The Sea is the biggest station — the point where the channel turns inward. That is why the elbows and knees carry the points most often used for the whole channel rather than for a local problem.

Palpation rule

Every point sits in a hollow. Run a fingertip along the bone or tendon until it drops into a soft depression; that dip is the point. If the skin feels tight or the finger skates over a hard ridge, you are on the bone, not in the hollow.

Part III · The body map · Module 09

The 30-second read

By the end you can open any channel chart and name its five points, their elements and its horary point in under a minute.

Everything in the course now folds into one routine. Run these five steps in order and you never need to memorise sixty points. You need the method, plus the landmark map from module 8.

  1. Read the channel. Yin or yang, and which element does the channel itself belong to?
  2. Set the start. Yin begins at Wood on the Well; yang begins at Metal on the Well.
  3. Walk the cycle. Step once per station along the generating cycle: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water.
  4. Mark the horary. The station whose element matches the channel's element is the horary point.
  5. Place it. Take the station from module 8, find the hollow, then open the chart to confirm.

Two worked reads

Lung. Yin, and the channel is Metal. Yin starts at Wood, so the cycle runs straight across the stations.

Lung · Yin · Metal
LU11 Well · Wood→LU10 Spring · Fire→LU9 Stream · Earth→LU8 River · Metal→LU5 Sea · Water

Metal lands on the River, so LU8 Jingqu is the horary point. Place it one cun above the wrist, on the radial side of the forearm.

Small Intestine. Yang, and the channel is Fire. Yang starts at Metal.

Small Intestine · Yang · Fire
SI1 Well · Metal→SI2 Spring · Water→SI3 Stream · Wood→SI5 River · Fire→SI8 Sea · Earth

Fire lands on the River, so SI5 Yanggu is the horary point, in the hollow on the ulnar side of the wrist. A yin channel and a yang channel, two different targets, one method.

Run the same five steps across the rest of the twelve channels and the whole atlas opens. The hard part is not the arithmetic; it is trusting that the method works when you meet an unfamiliar channel.

The course checklist

  • I can say the five stations in order and give the movement verb for each.
  • I can rebuild any channel's five point elements from its polarity and element.
  • I can name the horary point of all twelve channels.
  • I can find one point at each station on my own arm or leg from the landmark alone.
